# Institutional content, authorship and sharing

This is presentation-authoring guidance, **not a claim about TashTech's official approval procedure or information-classification policy**. Follow any supplied university policy and approval chain. A consistent logo does not turn a statement into an official university position.

## Establish the status of each material claim

| Status | Present it as | Evidence / boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Verified institutional fact | A sourced statement with date/context when relevant | Supplied approved material or a checked current primary source; cite it |
| Approved institutional statement | An institutional statement only within its approved scope | Explicit supplied approval; do not infer approval from branding or a person's title |
| Proposal or recommendation | “Proposed”, “For discussion” or “Recommendation” | Distinguish a suggested action, target, resource or owner from a commitment |
| Preliminary research | A preliminary result with its methods and limits | Actual underlying evidence; avoid overstating scope or certainty |
| Student work | “Student project”, “Course project” or equivalent | Name authors only when supplied; do not imply university endorsement |
| Synthetic example | “Illustrative example” / “Synthetic data” on the relevant slide | Do not use it as proof of real performance or institutional achievement |
| Not yet verified | A draft requiring verification, or omit the claim | Record the evidence gap; never fill it with a plausible invented fact |

Labels must be visible where misinterpretation could occur, including exported pages. A disclaimer hidden in speaker notes or only on the final slide is inadequate for a misleading chart. Approved wording supplied for a specific context should not be generalized into unrelated promises.

## Internal and external versions

Before an external delivery, review the entire HTML source, visible slides, notes, hidden answers, linked material and embedded images. Remove confidential budgets, personal records, private correspondence, unpublished data and unapproved claims that are not authorized for that audience. A PDF export omits notes, but it is not an automatic confidentiality review; visible content and image details still matter.

A label such as “Internal working draft” is **not access control**, encryption, permission enforcement or protection against forwarding. Do not advertise it as such. Generate a separate audience-safe copy when different groups need different information. Do not depend on hiding a slide or closing an answer panel.

Speaker notes use the same display as the presentation in this runtime. They are not private when the screen is shared. HTML recipients can inspect all hidden notes and answers.

## Avoid false authority

Do not fabricate a rector's signature, approval stamp, accreditation seal, department identity, event sponsor list or partner logo. Use the provided university marks with the asset rules; any new third-party mark requires a supplied or verified authorized source and appropriate use. A logo appearance does not establish a signed partnership.

A student opinion or speculative departmental proposal may use the shared design where authorized, but must be distinguishable from an institution-wide statement. No template flag or layout can certify that authorization. When a user asks for an official presentation but has not supplied supporting claims or approval, prepare an explicitly reviewable draft and explain what must be confirmed.

## Changeable claims require current sources

Programs, application deadlines, tuition/fees, scholarships, accreditation, rankings, partner status, office-holders, contacts, event schedules and regulations can change. Use current primary material when these are needed. Record the source and relevant date; if access is unavailable, omit the claim or flag it rather than asserting a remembered value. Preserve qualifying conditions and do not convert a proposal into a guaranteed benefit.

For reports, show reporting periods and definitions. Distinguish actuals, targets, forecasts and examples. Do not substitute a convenient denominator, invent a baseline or imply causation from an unlabeled comparison.

## Example disclosure language

- “For discussion — proposed scope, subject to review.”
- “Illustrative planning scenario — not a university performance report.”
- “Student project — views and conclusions of the project authors.”
- “Synthetic data for layout demonstration; not a research finding.”
- “Internal working draft — not approved for external release.”

Use only a label that accurately describes the content and known status. A label is not a substitute for removing information that must not be shared.

## Pre-delivery review

Check who is speaking, the intended audience, material-claim sources, status labels, current dates/conditions, author attribution and the absence of unauthorized sensitive content. Confirm which file is suitable for which audience. Read the actual output and PDF, not just the source outline. Never email, publish, host, install for other users or change access permissions without an explicit request.
